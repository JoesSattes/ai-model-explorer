\subsection{RQ6: Popular checkpoints on the Hub}
\label{sec:hub}

The corpus measures accuracy against independent labels, but its manifests come from one library's default configurations. To test the method on checkpoints as they are actually published, we selected 201 popular public repositories across eleven groups: dense and mixture-of-experts language models, state-space and hybrid models, encoders and embedding models, sequence-to-sequence models, speech and audio models, vision backbones, vision--language models, image and video generators, time-series and tabular models, and quantized releases. Gated models were taken from ungated mirrors where one exists. Together the repositories hold 12.1\,TB of weights and were downloaded about 505 million times in the month before the run. A benchmark page in the explorer (\tn{bench.html}) runs the same loader as the tool in the user's browser and exports each manifest, the configuration files and the Hub's metadata; the analysis then runs offline with the same recovery code.

191 repositories were read. Of the ten failures, seven are gated, two store old non-zip pickles, and one, MMS-1B-all, ships one adapter file per language, and reading all their headers triggered the Hub's rate limit; the loader now skips such adapters. Reading the 191 manifests transferred 161\,MB in total. The median repository needed 79\,KB, the 95th percentile 5.4\,MB, and the largest, an NVFP4 release of DeepSeek-R1 with 163 shards and 180{,}488 tensors, 20.8\,MB. Recovery took 18\,ms for the median model and 4.2\,s for the largest.

\paragraph{Parameter counts.} The Hub's model pages show a parameter count computed from safetensors metadata, which gives an independent check of the header reading. Excluding 0-d scalars, which the Hub skips, our count equals the Hub's for 151 of the 158 transformers-style repositories that have one: the stored count for full-precision files, and the logical count for FP8, GPTQ, AWQ, MXFP4 and MLX releases, for which the Hub reports logical sizes. For the 12 diffusers pipelines with a count, the Hub's figure equals exactly the parameters we assign to the denoiser (the UNet or transformer folder), while our total also includes the text encoders and VAE. Of the seven remaining differences, four are 4-bit releases where the Hub counts packed storage: an NVFP4 DeepSeek-R1 is shown at 396.8\,B instead of 684.5\,B (Table~\ref{tab:hub}), NVFP4 8\,B models are shown at about 57\% of their size, and bitsandbytes releases 2 to 3\% above it, because packed bytes, block scales and quantization maps are counted as parameters. The other three come from extra files that the Hub does not count, such as a codec stored beside a speech model and LoRA folders shipped with Phi-4-multimodal.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters and roles.} Table~\ref{tab:hubhp} repeats the analysis of RQ4 on the Hub checkpoints. The pattern is the same as on the corpus, with higher numbers because popular checkpoints are mostly single-stack models with standard layouts. From the manifest alone, the head count is right for 77\% of models; with fusion it is right for 97\%, and the configuration agrees with the weights for 140 of the 143 checked models. For 82 checkpoints whose model type is also in the corpus, we transferred the class-name labels by index-masked tensor name. Role accuracy on these is 96.8\% macro, and 71 of the 82 reach 99\%. The four below 90\% are xLSTM, ALBERT and Zamba2, which also fail on the corpus, and RT-DETR, whose CNN backbone the rules split into many small stacks. Table~\ref{tab:hub-groups} breaks the results down by group, and Appendix~\ref{app:hub} lists every checkpoint.

\paragraph{What real checkpoints added.} Four problems appeared only on real files, and each led to a change that was then checked on the corpus. DeepSeek-V3 and GLM-4.5 store their multi-token-prediction layer as one extra item of the main layer list. It is now recognized by its $[d, 2d]$ projection and two extra norms and reported separately, so the layer count matches the configuration (61 and 92 rather than 62 and 93). bitsandbytes releases keep the logical shape in a serialized state blob that the header does not expose; the loader now regroups the state tensors with their weight, counts two values per packed byte, and rebuilds a matrix shape from the residual width, which recovers the 8.19\,B of Qwen3-8B. In Qwen3-0.6B the key and value projections are square $[d,d]$, which had made one of them look like the output projection; the output is now taken from the non-square side. Whisper's encoder stores a position table with 1{,}500 rows that had been read as a token table, which made the encoder look like the backbone. A component fed by a convolutional stem is no longer treated as having a token table. On the loading side, repositories that ship several copies of the same weights (FP16 and FP32 files, single-file checkpoints beside a diffusers layout, LoRA files) are now reduced to one description, because the first run read some of them more than once.
